Interactive Experience Research Project

Website - Luni App

Keira Izzard

1. What was the first thing you paid attention to when interacting with the experience?

Upon first opening the Luni app website, my focus was immediately drawn to the increasing numbers in the right corner. This short animation built anticipation and effortlessly flowed into the home screen of the website. The seamless transitions made it feel as if I was slowly flying through the animations. This, coupled with the soft minimalist style, kept me consistently engaged.

2. Discrete actions I uncovered

During this short interaction, I scrolled down on the page, hovered over animations, clicked through options, paused to see animations unfold and scrolled back up to rewatch animations appear.

3. What part of the experience did you spend the most time engaging with?

When interacting with the Luni website, I spent the most time engaging with the scrolling animation sections. This element held my focus due to its calming visual effect and effortless change depending on scrolling down.

4. What was the most common action in your two minute interaction with the experience?

The most common action used within my experience was scrolling almost every element relied on a scroll to reveal new content. This allowed me to trigger animations, engage with interactive content, and guided me through the website, navigating me to key sections.

5. What is your impression of the intended primary goal of the interactive experience?

After viewing Luni App, my impression of the intended primary goal of the interactive experience is to illustrate the product in an immersive and visual way, encouraging participants to actively engage in the exploration rather than just reading content.

6. How does the interactive experience communicate this primary goal?

The experience conveys this goal through its heavy integration of animation, graphics and creative transitions. In using these elements, the website has reduced its text to minimal, and rejected traditional web layouts. Allowing it to focus on movements and flow to guide the user through the experience.

7. What is your impression of how the experience should be interacted with over time? (For how long and how many different times)

The unique immersive experience provided by Luni, I believe, is designed to be interacted with in a short timeframe, but often with repetition, with users choosing to potentially revisit content to trigger animations again. It feels like a quick attention grab, but something that users would truly engage with again.

8. How does the interactive experience communicate how it should be interacted with over time?

This is communicated through the layout of the site, a minimalist style paired with a strong visual animative element, which implies fast-paced user engagement. The smooth transition and navigation of the website confirms a desire for a all in one interaction, allowing the user to get from A to B in one sitting. This here suggests that the Luni app website is to be consumed in a casual, quick manner as opposed to a more lengthy session.

9. What other media forms (digital or otherwise) does the experience reference?

The experience references other media forms such as mobile app interfaces, digital products, and motion design, commonly associated with branding for new tech releases.

10. What does this reference/s communicate to you about how you should act when engaging with your research experience?

Luni's references communicate that users should engage with the website’s content in a hands-on and exploratory way. This method emulates how I would engage with a new digital product, which strongly aligns with Luni's goal.
